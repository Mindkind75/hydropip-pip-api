# HydroPip Site Planning and Common Questions

## What a four-tower HydroPip needs

Use the shared Site Planning calculator for dimensions. Its standard four-tower working envelope is approximately 12 by 8 feet when the 275-gallon IBC is placed behind or beside the tower line. This is a planning envelope, not a guaranteed minimum. It includes mature foliage and a practical service route. The compact equipment envelope is smaller, but tight layouts are harder to prune, harvest, flush, inspect, and reset.

Plan tower centers about 3 feet apart. Reserve roughly 3 feet of usable service access where the grower must reach the IBC opening, pumps, hoses, flush valve, and back or sides of the towers. Mark the complete layout on the ground before driving supports.

## Choosing the yard location

Prefer a level, stable, well-drained area with convenient water and GFCI-protected outdoor power. Leafy crops generally need at least 6 hours of useful direct light; fruiting crops usually perform better near 8 hours. In very hot climates, morning sun with some late-afternoon relief may be easier to manage than reflected all-day heat. Keep the IBC covered or shaded to reduce light-driven algae and reservoir heating without shading the crop canopy.

Avoid low spots, standing water, roof runoff, vehicle paths, septic areas, private irrigation, and buried utilities. Before driving any support, call 811 or visit https://call811.com/, wait for public utilities to be marked, and separately identify private water, irrigation, septic, and electrical lines.

Wind matters. Use deeper anchoring and the 10-foot galvanized support option in exposed locations. Do not put mature towers where they block doors, gates, HVAC service, emergency access, or safe movement through the yard.

## What Pip needs to check a space

For a useful text check, collect tower count, available width and depth, approximate direct-sun hours, crop type, wind exposure, drainage, water access, GFCI power access, and any fence, wall, roof, tree, or walkway obstruction.

A free HydroPip member may attach a proposed-site photo as one of the five complimentary Build Checks. Ask for a wide photo showing the whole location, then provide available width and depth, compass direction or morning/afternoon sun pattern, and where the IBC would sit. A single photo cannot prove exact dimensions, underground safety, drainage performance, or all-day sun exposure. Pip must separate visible evidence from facts that still need confirmation.

Photo site guidance for DWC, NFT, Kratky, Dutch bucket, aeroponic, recirculating, or other custom systems is a Pip Pro feature. Free placement help remains available for the HydroPip timed-feed tower design.

## Common DIY hydroponic questions

### How much light does it need?

HydroPip outdoor leafy crops should begin with a location receiving about 6 or more hours of direct light. Fruiting crops generally benefit from about 8 hours. Excess reflected heat can still stress plants, so consider crop choice, reservoir shade, and afternoon conditions rather than judging only by total sun hours.

### How close should the towers be?

Plan approximately 3 feet center-to-center for the standard build. Mature foliage can extend beyond the planter body. Preserve a service path rather than packing the empty towers as tightly as possible.

### Where should the IBC go?

Put it on a stable, level base close enough for practical pump head and hose routing. Keep the top opening, mixing return, pumps, and controls reachable. Use an opaque cover and avoid intense reflected heat. Do not put plugs or connections where runoff or rain can collect.

### What crops should a beginner start with?

Leaf lettuce, chard, kale, basil, parsley, and cilantro are practical first crops. Pip should use the saved growing zone and month for timing. Large fruiting crops need more support, canopy room, light, and nutrient demand.

### What pH and meters are required?

pH monitoring is a useful basic practice. EC or TDS is optional optimization for the basic HydroPip method. Use meter readings with plant appearance, water level, flow, and the current nutrient batch instead of correcting a number blindly.

### Why is algae forming?

Algae needs light, moisture, and nutrients. Block light from the IBC and wet media surfaces, close unnecessary openings, clean visible growth, and verify that covers do not interfere with circulation or access.

### What causes brown or unhealthy roots?

Check water temperature, oxygen and circulation, pump operation, over-wet media, odor, and sanitation. Brown staining is not always rot, but foul odor, slime, and collapsing roots require prompt attention.

### What if a pump stops?

Verify GFCI power, smart-plug state, intake blockage, water level, hose kinks, and pump operation. Keep the two pump roles separate. A spare compatible pump reduces crop risk once plants are established.

### How should feeding be tuned?

Start with short timed feeds and inspect a representative tower for media moisture and light runoff. Hot weather and mature plants may need more frequent short feeds; cool weather may need fewer. Do not add runoff-return plumbing to the standard HydroPip build.

### How do I control pests?

Identify the pest before selecting a treatment. Inspect leaf undersides, remove badly affected material, improve sanitation and airflow, and follow every pesticide label, crop restriction, and pre-harvest interval. Pip Pro photo guidance can help narrow a plant or pest problem.

### How do I handle heat and storms?

Shade or insulate the IBC without blocking crop light, monitor reservoir temperature, secure hoses and covers, and use stronger stabilization in exposed sites. Disconnect outdoor electrical equipment when conditions are unsafe. Use only outdoor-rated, GFCI-protected equipment and keep connections elevated and dry.

### Can the system expand later?

Yes. Begin with fewer towers if needed, leave hose length and an end connector for expansion, and confirm pump flow and head before adding towers. Recheck service access and support locations instead of simply filling every open space.

### How is the system cleaned and reset?

At crop turnover, remove plants, recover reusable perlite and vermiculite over a screen, clean pots and hoses, inspect pumps, and reset the towers. Healthy mature plants may be moved to suitable raised beds when climate, crop, and root condition allow.

## Guidance basis

This question set reflects recurring home-hydroponic concerns documented by university extension guidance: light, water, nutrients, oxygen, pH, algae, sanitation, pests, root health, system access, and safe operation. Pip should answer the user's immediate question first, apply HydroPip-specific construction facts, and ask only for the next missing measurement or observation.
